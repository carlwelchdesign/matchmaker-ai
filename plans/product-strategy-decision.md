# ARG-001 Product Strategy Decision Packet

## Decision requested

Approve, revise, or reject the four recommendations below. Approval establishes
the product-strategy boundary needed for founder/matchmaker research and concept
testing. It does not approve pricing, contracts, admission criteria, legal
language, campaign copy, or implementation.

## Confirmed direction

These points already have explicit founder direction or an accepted project
decision:

- Argent Matchmaking is the company and platform brand.
- Anyone may apply, but admission is selective and human-reviewed.
- Initial positioning is affluent, high-end, discreet matchmaking.
- Argent controls the platform and candidate network.
- Partners participate through controlled, co-branded Argent campaigns, not
  self-service tenant software.
- The initial proving ground is Santa Barbara County/Montecito.
- The service is human-led; AI cannot autonomously admit, reject, rank, or
  introduce people.
- The experience avoids public profiles, swiping, popularity mechanics, and
  predictive compatibility claims.

## Recommendation 1 — Beachhead client

Approve this initial ideal client profile:

> An adult, relationship-intentional professional, founder, executive, or
> similarly time-constrained person in or connected to Santa Barbara County who
> can afford a high-touch service, materially values privacy, accepts a
> human-led process, and is willing to give candid feedback without expecting a
> guaranteed romantic outcome.

Why:

- It describes the problem Argent solves—time, privacy, curation, and
  accountability—rather than treating wealth or occupation as character.
- It is narrow enough for one matchmaker and a local pilot.
- It does not encode gender, presumed narcissism, public status, or net worth as
  a match-quality signal.

Not approved by this recommendation:

- a minimum income/net-worth rule;
- gender-specific service promises;
- a claim that affluent people are more suitable partners;
- national or international search;
- public-figure-grade security or concierge promises before operations support
  them.

**Owner decision:** Approve / revise / reject.

## Recommendation 2 — Who pays

Approve a client-funded concierge model for the pilot:

- Paying clients retain Argent for a defined human-led search and service
  process.
- Candidates may apply to the private network without paying to be considered
  or matched.
- Candidate status does not include a dedicated matchmaker, a promised
  introduction, or a guaranteed response beyond the approved service policy.
- Campaign partners do not pay for profile access. Any campaign sponsorship,
  referral compensation, or service fee remains a later contract/economics
  decision.

Do not charge the proposed `$500` candidate interview/database fee in the first
pilot. It risks confusing payment with acceptance, access, endorsement, or
match priority before Argent has validated demand and a specific delivered
candidate service. If a candidate-paid service is later tested, it needs its
own deliverable, price, refund terms, disclosure, and proof that non-payment
does not reduce matching treatment.

Why:

- It aligns the payer with the party receiving dedicated search labor.
- It keeps the candidate pool broad enough for a local pilot.
- It avoids pay-to-be-considered and paid-placement trust problems.
- It mirrors a visible category pattern without requiring Argent to copy a
  competitor's package or claims.

**Owner decision:** Approve / revise / reject.

## Recommendation 3 — Pilot service promise

Approve this promise boundary:

> Argent provides a confidential, human-led intake, criteria definition,
> candidate search, screening coordination, curated introduction process, and
> post-introduction feedback loop for a defined engagement term.

Argent may promise the process it controls:

- a named point of contact and response expectations;
- a documented search scope and active term;
- agreed intake and criteria work;
- human review of potential introductions;
- mutual permission before identifying or connecting people;
- feedback and service-recovery steps;
- privacy, withdrawal, and support paths.

Argent must not promise:

- admission to the candidate network;
- a fixed number of suitable people before capacity is known;
- a match, date, repeat date, relationship, engagement, or marriage;
- complete verification or safety;
- that payment, an invite, wealth, or a partner referral improves ranking;
- that software or an assessment predicts chemistry or relationship success.

Exact package, term, price, hold time, cancellation, refund, staffing capacity,
and remedies remain `ARG-010`/`ARG-017` decisions.

**Owner decision:** Approve / revise / reject.

## Recommendation 4 — Proof before expansion

Approve one small operational-alpha hypothesis:

> With synthetic cases first and then an explicitly consenting local cohort,
> one Argent matchmaker can use a private candidate network and controlled
> campaign to produce mutually approved introductions for a small number of
> paying pilot clients with acceptable service effort, participant experience,
> privacy, and safety.

Use the “100 bachelors and 100 bachelorettes” concept only as a provisional
campaign capacity/recruiting target. It is not a balanced-gender product
requirement, a success claim, or a reason to collect unnecessary people.

Evidence categories for the later metric ticket:

- demand: qualified client conversations and willingness to pay;
- supply: current, consenting candidates who satisfy actual client constraints;
- service: time to review, shortlist, introduction, and follow-up;
- participant control: mutual approval, withdrawal, correction, and support;
- experience: client and candidate satisfaction with response quality and
  discretion;
- outcomes: first meeting and interest in another meeting, reported separately
  with missing feedback visible;
- trust: privacy, security, safety, complaint, and misleading-expectation
  incidents;
- economics: matchmaker hours, recruiting cost, and gross contribution per
  engagement.

Thresholds, sample size, cohort rules, and stop/iterate/expand criteria remain
`ARG-011`.

**Owner decision:** Approve / revise / reject.

## Current-market check

This is a positioning check, not evidence that competitor claims are true or
that their models work:

- Tawkify distinguishes paying clients with a dedicated matchmaker from members
  in its candidate pool and says packages vary by features and match count.
  [Official FAQ](https://tawkify.com/faq)
- Selective Search describes a retained client search plus a complimentary,
  confidential candidate program with no promised introduction.
  [Client service](https://www.selectivesearch.com/matchmaker-services-for-clients/)
  and
  [candidate program](https://www.selectivesearch.com/candidate-program/)
- Kelleher describes custom high-touch packages, manual selection, private
  profiles, and a multi-person matchmaking team.
  [Official service overview](https://kelleher-international.com/)
- Raya's terms illustrate selective application and waitlist mechanics, but
  Raya is a membership app rather than Argent's proposed concierge service.
  [Official terms](https://www.raya.app/termsofservice)

Competitor success percentages, network sizes, verification claims, and
testimonials are self-reported marketing and must not become Argent claims or
planning assumptions without independently reviewable definitions and
evidence.

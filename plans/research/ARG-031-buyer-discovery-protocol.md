## Decision this research supports

Determine whether a narrowly defined paying-client segment has a recent,
material problem that Argent's human-led concierge process could address, has
purchase authority and a plausible budget path, sees a meaningful gap in
current alternatives, and will take a credible next step toward a bounded pilot.

The research may support a decision to continue, revise, or stop buyer and offer
development. It cannot approve pricing, contracts, outreach, product
implementation, matchmaking claims, or a production launch.

## Hard authorization boundary

This file is a planning artifact, not permission to recruit. Do not identify,
contact, screen, schedule, interview, take notes about, quote, record,
transcribe, follow up with, or store information about a real participant until:

1. shared research controls are approved and operations are `Open`;
2. the ARG-031 protocol and exact activity are approved in
   `research-authorization.json`;
3. the approved system of record, recruitment channels, consent readout,
   withdrawal path, accessibility path, incentive decision, and safety owners
   are available; and
4. the researcher verifies that every dated approval is current.

Synthetic rehearsal may test the guide and templates but must be labeled
`Synthetic`, cannot use a real person's history, and cannot count as demand
evidence.

## Research questions

1. What recent event made the participant seek or seriously consider help with
   finding a long-term partner?
2. What have they already tried, what did those alternatives cost in time,
   money, privacy, or confidence, and what remained unresolved?
3. Who decides whether to purchase a high-touch service, what information is
   needed, and what constraints govern that decision?
4. Which process qualities—human judgment, discretion, candidate access,
   accountability, local knowledge, or another factor—would justify switching?
5. Which promises or data practices create distrust or make the service
   unacceptable?
6. What concrete, reversible next step will they take after seeing an approved,
   bounded pilot proposition?

## Cohorts and classification

### Primary cohort: practicing prospective paying clients

An eligible primary participant must:

- be an adult who, within the previous 24 months, actively sought a long-term
  relationship and tried or seriously evaluated a paid, human-led matchmaking
  or curated-introduction service;
- be the purchaser or materially participate in the purchase decision;
- be able to discuss service-selection behavior without disclosing intimate
  romantic details, exact wealth, identity documents, or third-party personal
  information; and
- be willing to evaluate a high-touch, human-led matchmaking proposition
  without expecting a guaranteed introduction or romantic outcome.

Santa Barbara County/Montecito relevance is useful for the first pilot but is
not an eligibility requirement or a permanent ICP boundary.

### Adjacent/proxy cohort

Founders, matchmakers, referral partners, service operators, and people who can
describe a buyer but do not personally meet the primary criteria are proxies.
Their evidence may explain operations or language but cannot satisfy buyer
thresholds.

People with experience purchasing analogous high-touch services—such as
executive search, private wealth advisory, concierge medicine, executive
coaching, or luxury travel advisory—but no recent matchmaking purchase
evaluation are also proxies. Their buying-process evidence must remain separate
from direct matchmaking demand evidence.

### Exclusions

Exclude a person when participation would require:

- sharing intimate dating or relationship history;
- sharing exact income, net worth, account balances, identity documents, or
  payment credentials;
- naming or describing identifiable third parties;
- receiving legal, clinical, safety, relationship, or emergency advice;
- resolving an unmanaged conflict of interest; or
- recording or transcription to participate.

## Sample rationale and stopping rule

This is a small qualitative decision sample, not a statistically representative
market survey.

- Target: 12 completed primary-cohort sessions.
- Minimum evaluable sample: 8 primary-cohort sessions.
- Adjacent/proxy cap: up to 4 sessions, analyzed separately and never counted
  toward primary thresholds.
- Review points: after 4, 8, and 12 primary sessions.
- Stop at 12 primary sessions unless the owner approves a documented extension
  for a specific unresolved segment contradiction.

Do not stop early merely because positive themes repeat. At the first review
point, deliberately seek disconfirming participants and alternatives.

## Proposed recruitment-source candidates

These are candidates for later approval, not approved channels:

- warm introductions from people who do not receive outcome-based compensation;
- opt-in outreach to an owner-controlled professional network;
- introductions from high-trust service professionals under an approved
  no-disclosure/no-endorsement boundary; and
- an approved public research-interest form that is distinct from an Argent
  application or candidate intake.

Recruitment language must say that participation is research, not an
application, verification, admission decision, referral endorsement, or promise
of service. No list, name, address, handle, or contact detail belongs in this
repository.

## Minimized screener

Ask only after outreach and screening are separately authorized.

1. In the last 24 months, have you actively sought a long-term relationship and
   paid for or seriously evaluated a human-led matchmaking or
   curated-introduction service?
2. Did you purchase, seriously evaluate, or reject that service? Record only
   the broad action, not personal relationship details.
3. Were you the purchaser, a joint decision-maker, an adviser, or not involved
   in the purchase decision?
4. Could you discuss how you evaluated the service without sharing names,
   intimate history, exact finances, or confidential third-party information?
5. Would you be comfortable reviewing a bounded, human-led matchmaking service
   concept that makes no outcome guarantees?
6. Is an accessible format, communication method, scheduling adjustment, or
   interaction adjustment needed? Answering is optional; record only the action
   needed, not a diagnosis.

Do not ask for exact income, net worth, relationship history, protected-class
attributes, health information, identity documents, or the identity of a
partner, candidate, adviser, or service provider.

## Interview guide

### Opening and recent behavior

1. Think about the most recent time you sought or seriously considered a
   human-led matchmaking or curated-introduction service. Without sharing
   intimate details, what happened immediately before you began looking?
2. What did you do first? What did you try next?
3. What made the problem important enough to spend time or money addressing?
4. What happened when you delayed or did nothing?

### Alternatives and unresolved cost

5. Which alternatives did you compare, including doing it yourself or asking
   your network?
6. How did you decide which alternatives were credible?
7. Without sharing exact finances, where did the alternative feel inexpensive,
   considered, or substantial relative to the problem?
8. What remained unresolved after using or rejecting those alternatives?

### Authority and buying process

9. Who was involved in the decision and who could stop it?
10. What evidence, terms, references, privacy information, or service details
    did you need before committing?
11. Was there an existing budget, a discretionary decision, or a new approval
    process? Do not request an exact amount.
12. What made you trust or distrust the provider?

### Switching triggers and Argent boundary

13. What would have to be true for you to switch from your current approach?
14. Which part of a confidential, human-led matchmaking process would be most
    valuable, and which part would create concern?
15. What would “selective,” “private,” “verified,” or “curated” need to mean to
    avoid misleading you?
16. Which promise would immediately make the service feel implausible or unsafe?

### Commitment ladder

Only present an owner-approved, non-transactional pilot proposition. Ask for the
smallest honest next step; do not pressure escalation.

| Level | Observable response | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | General positive reaction only | Hypothetical interest; no commitment evidence |
| 1 | Identifies the information or conditions needed to evaluate an offer | Concrete consideration criteria; no follow-up commitment |
| 2 | Independently grants protocol-specific follow-up to review a separately approved offer artifact | Reversible time commitment; not purchase intent |

Level 2 may be measured only when `followUp` is authorized and the exact offer
artifact and review script are versioned, pinned, and approved. Until then, the
highest valid score is level 1. No deposit, payment link, contract, signature,
application, decision-maker referral, or candidate intake is allowed under this
protocol. Declining a next step must not affect incentives, network eligibility,
or future treatment.

Every primary session included in the 8- or 12-session decision denominator
must use the same approved offer-artifact and review-script revision and must
have `followUp` permission available for an independently granted level-2
choice. Discovery-only rehearsals or sessions without that approval may refine
the guide but cannot enter the decision denominator.

## Evidence coding

For each primary session, code independently:

- `recentProblem`: concrete event within 24 months / vague or older / absent;
- `currentAlternative`: named category and action / considered only / absent;
- `unresolvedCost`: concrete time, privacy, confidence, or broad spend band /
  vague / absent;
- `purchaseAuthority`: sole / joint / adviser / none / unknown;
- `switchingTrigger`: observable condition / preference only / absent;
- `trustConstraint`: concrete / vague / absent;
- `commitmentLevel`: 0–2 with the supporting action; and
- `disconfirmingEvidence`: contradiction, refusal, acceptable alternative, or
  reason the problem should not be solved by Argent.

Do not infer wealth, relationship suitability, urgency, willingness to pay, or
commitment from occupation, location, demeanor, status, or a positive quote.

## Predeclared decision thresholds

Evaluate thresholds only after at least 8 eligible primary sessions. Report
counts and denominators; do not convert this sample into market percentages.
Use the 8-session threshold at the first decision point and the 12-session
threshold when sampling continues.

### Pass to bounded offer and commerce design

All must be true. At 8 primary sessions, the required counts are 6, 5, 5, and 4
respectively. At 12 primary sessions, they are 9, 8, 8, and 6:

- concrete recent problem and action already taken;
- material unresolved cost and a credible switching trigger;
- sole or joint purchase authority;
- commitment level 2;
- proxy and analogous-service evidence are excluded from every threshold; and
- no recurring critical privacy, safety, deception, or outcome-guarantee
  expectation makes the proposed service boundary untenable.

Passing permits a recommendation to design and review a concrete offer. It does
not validate a price, demonstrate purchase intent, authorize sales, or prove
product-market fit.

### Revise and retest

Use `Revise` when the minimum sample is met but one or more pass thresholds miss
without meeting a stop condition, evidence splits materially by cohort, the
buying process is unclear, or participants require a materially different
service promise. The recommendation must name the changed hypothesis and the
smallest new evidence needed.

### Stop or reconsider the beachhead

Recommend `Stop` when any is true:

- fewer than 3 of 8 primary participants describe a concrete recent problem and
  action, or fewer than 4 of 12 when sampling continues;
- fewer than 2 of 8 reach commitment level 2, or fewer than 3 of 12 when
  sampling continues;
- a readily available alternative resolves the problem for at least 6 of 8, or
  at least 9 of 12 when sampling continues;
- credible demand depends on guarantees, surveillance, discriminatory
  selection, undisclosed data sharing, or pay-to-rank treatment; or
- the required privacy, safety, or service burden is incompatible with the
  bounded pilot.

## Required disconfirming-evidence search

The synthesis must actively include:

- people who considered and rejected high-touch help;
- satisfactory existing alternatives;
- low urgency or no recent action;
- lack of purchase authority;
- reasons not to trust a private matchmaking service;
- refusal of follow-up or commitment despite positive concept language;
- segment differences hidden by aggregate counts; and
- evidence that Argent would create rather than reduce effort or risk.

## Evidence and synthesis rules

- Use `buyer-discovery-evidence-ledger.md` with opaque references only.
- Use `buyer-discovery-synthesis.md` for the 4/8/12-session reviews.
- Complete every numeric scorecard result and qualitative decision gate in the
  synthesis. A qualitative `Yes` requires claim-level `EVD-` ledger evidence
  IDs, not participant references; these gates override a numeric pass
  according to the predeclared decision rules.
- Use the exact pinned versions from the protocol authorization; changing a
  template requires a new protocol revision and approval.
- Preserve negative and contradictory evidence; never select only supportive
  quotes.
- Separate observation, participant interpretation, researcher interpretation,
  and recommendation.
- Short quotes require independent permission and redaction. Paraphrase by
  default.
- Synthetic rehearsals use IDs prefixed `SYN-` and stay outside all threshold
  denominators.
- Repository artifacts must never contain names, contact details, intimate
  history, exact finances, recordings, raw transcripts, or payment data.

## Completion evidence required before `Done`

ARG-031 cannot be marked `Done` from the protocol or a narrative summary alone.
The machine record must pin:

- an immutable authorization snapshot showing `Open` operations, an `Approved`
  protocol, exact allowed activities, approval evidence, and the effective

- at least 8 eligible primary sessions;
- the completed evidence ledger and withdrawal/exclusion disposition artifact;
- the completed synthesis with protocol-version, session-count, threshold, and

- a dated `Pass`, `Revise`, or `Stop` owner decision with evidence.

Expired approvals after the sessions do not erase valid historical evidence,
but missing proof that sessions occurred inside an authorized window blocks
completion. External Asana/GitHub evidence references require human verification
before completion; repository references are resolved by validation.
